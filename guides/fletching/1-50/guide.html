Arrows to 30 → Willow Bow to 45 → Maple Bow to 50. If feathers are slow to obtain, use Oak Bow from level 15.

Copper Arrows from 5 to 10 offer a very small advantage in the reference scenario. Keeping Headless Arrows simplifies supplies. This practical route does not attempt to find the exact action sequence that saves the last few seconds.

The key condition: feathers

The example assumes 30 seconds of total time per Giant Chicken, including respawn and allocated time to replenish consumables. A 50% chance to receive 40–70 feathers gives 27.5 feathers per kill on average: about 1.09 s per feather in this scenario. The 30 seconds are an assumption, not a verified combat rate.

From levels 15 to 25, Iron Arrows and Oak Bow are nearly tied. Keeping other base values, Oak Bow wins if each feather takes more than about 1.21 s, equivalent to a Giant Chicken cycle above about 33 s.

  • Fast feathers: keep Iron Arrows to 25.
  • Slow feathers: switch to Oak Bow at 15; its full chain yields about 2,880 XP/h.
  • At 25: Steel Arrows improve efficiency. If feathers cost more than about 8.75 s each, Steel Javelin takes the lead from level 26, keeping other base rates.

Hunting also provides feathers, but requires surveying, tracking, capture attempts, and traps where applicable. The capture interval alone does not represent total gathering time.

Bow preparation

  1. Cut the corresponding logs.
  2. Gather Flax from Flax Bush.
  3. Turn Flax into Bowstring with Crafting, or use the shop exchange if already unlocked.
  4. Craft Willow Bow to 45, then Maple Bow to 50.

The thresholds used are 13,362 XP at level 30, 61,510 at 45, and 101,331 at 50. Route batches total 101,382 XP. Stop at the desired level if bonuses reduce consumption. XP table.

Why crossbows stop being the priority

Final crossbow crafting grants plenty of XP, but its components use several bars. When everything must be mined, smelted, and crafted, that advantage shrinks.

Worked example: Willow Bow

2 Willow Logs: 10 s. Gather 1 Flax: 2 s. Craft 1 Bowstring: 2 s. Craft the bow: 10 s.

32 XP × 3,600 ÷ (10 + 2 + 2 + 10) = 4,800 XP/h

Worked example: Steel 2H Crossbow

Requires 6 Willow Logs, 2 Steel Crossbow Heads, and 1 Bowstring. The two heads consume 6 Steel Bars and 20 s of Smithing.

The six bars cost about 64.77 s of mining and smelting. Adding 30 s for wood, 20 s for the heads, 4 s for the string, and 16 s of Fletching gives about 134.77 s total.

121 XP × 3,600 ÷ 134.77 ≈ 3,232 XP/h

When to adjust the route

Flax for Bowstring exchange

The shop exchanges 1 Flax for 1 Bowstring, but requires Skilled Crafter in the Mastery Guild. Crafting level alone does not unlock this purchase.

If the exchange is already available, it removes 2 s per string. Across the 2,192 bows from levels 30–50, this saves about 1 h 13 min. The entire route falls to about 19 h 04 min, keeping other assumptions. Time to earn the rank is excluded: this comparison assumes it is already unlocked. Shop requirements.

Feathers Flax Basket

This item grants +15% doubling chance for Flax Bush. Its Beastcrafting 6 recipe uses 2 String, 2 Stick, and 10 feathers, with 6 s of final crafting. Gathering 2,192 Flax with the bonus saves about nine minutes before subtracting basket preparation. Recipe and bonus.

Stock, alternative sources, and parallel production

Ready-made components make crossbows more attractive for reaching the next level. If you need to replenish stock, include that cost again. Thieving and combat also supply some materials; their efficiency depends on success, equipment, drops, and consumable replenishment time.

Farming and Ranching can supply resources in parallel. Include only waiting that actually delays progression, plus the work and inputs needed to maintain production. The reference scenario uses active gathering and does not assume established farms or herds.

Gathering upgrades, Step Skipping, preservation, doubling, and extra XP require recalculating the chain. When buying an upgrade just for this goal, compare time saved up to 50 with the time needed to pay its resource and currency cost.

Research method and limitations

  • Auxiliary skills already meet the required levels; their leveling time is excluded.
  • Active gathering and crafting are added sequentially, starting without materials.
  • No equipment, potion, Astrology, event, or Mastery Guild bonuses in the base scenario.
  • Mining includes early successes and guaranteed breaks through rock progress.
  • Smelting and crafting tips, components, strings, shafts, and headless arrows are included.
  • Other skill XP is an additional benefit; the calculated objective is reaching Fletching 50.
  • Quest XP, new unlock costs, menu navigation, and idle time are excluded.
  • Ammunition calculations use the feather cost described above. Recalculate if your source has another speed.
  • Consulted databases include data extracted from build 26724394. The XP table identifies build 26886380. Game updates can change recipes or rates.

Effective XP/h = 3,600 × chain Fletching XP ÷ total chain seconds

For arrows, each final batch of 4 units receives another 1.6 Fletching XP allocated from earlier production: 0.4 XP from Arrow Shafts and 1.2 XP from Headless Arrows. The Iron Arrows chain therefore uses 8.6 XP per batch, and Steel Arrows uses 13.6 XP.

Mean mining time uses a capped geometric distribution: with break chance p, a 1.5 s interval, and a limit of n hits, the mean is 1.5 × [1 − (1 − p)n] ÷ p. Account for output per break when converting the result to time per item.
